# Skill: Subject / Book Writer

## Purpose

Write long-form content: articles, newsletters, mini-guides, and short ebooks for ByeByeAdmin. Designed for depth and credibility rather than quick social posts.

## When to use

- Writing a newsletter issue (weekly or monthly)
- Creating a lead magnet or resource for assessment completers
- Drafting a long LinkedIn article or industry piece
- Building a "book" or structured guide on a topic (e.g. "The Haulage Operator's Guide to Cutting Admin")

## Long-form article structure

```
HEADLINE: [Specific, outcome-focused. No clickbait.]

INTRO (150–200 words):
- Open with a concrete situation or statistic
- State the problem clearly
- Tell the reader what they'll get from reading

SECTION 1: [The problem in detail]
SECTION 2: [Why current approaches fail]
SECTION 3: [The solution / new approach]
SECTION 4: [How to implement it / examples]
CONCLUSION: [Summary + one CTA]
```

## Newsletter format

```
Subject: [Specific, direct — avoid "this week's newsletter"]

INTRO: 1–2 sentences. What's this issue about and why does it matter now.

MAIN PIECE: [300–500 words. One focused topic.]

QUICK HITS: [2–3 bullet points of short observations or links]

FROM THE WORK: [1 thing from client work this week — anonymised if needed]

CTA: [One ask — book a call, reply with a question, follow on YT]
```

## Writing principles

- Open with the most interesting sentence, not background
- Use subheadings every 300–400 words — readers scan first
- Include at least one specific example with a real number
- No em dashes
- Write for someone who reads on their phone while having lunch
- End sections with a bridge to the next one

## Lead magnet format

Short, high-value, practical. Aim for 1,500–3,000 words. Structure:
1. The problem (why this matters)
2. The framework (how to think about it)
3. The steps (how to do it)
4. The tools (what to use)
5. Next step CTA (assessment or call)
