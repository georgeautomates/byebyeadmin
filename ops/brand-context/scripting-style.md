# George's Scripting Style Guide

Derived from analysis of 31 real video transcripts. This is how George actually talks on camera, not how he should talk. Any script written for George must sound like these transcripts, not like marketing copy.

## Opening patterns (what actually works)

George's best-performing videos share specific opening structures. These are ranked by what actually drove views.

### 1. The "pain picture" open (top performer pattern)
Start by describing a specific Monday-morning-in-the-office scene the audience lives every day. Make them feel seen before offering anything.

> "If you're a haulage operator in the UK, then I know what your Monday morning looks like. 40-something new emails in your inbox, all with orders that need adding manually into your TMS."

> "What will your operations look like in 2026? The answer for most is the same as 2025. 60-hour work weeks, weekend invoicing, manual order entry..."

### 2. The "watch this" open (demo videos)
State the problem in one sentence, then immediately show the system working. No preamble.

> "Still typing orders into your TMS from emails? Here's me forwarding one to my AI agent instead. Watch what comes back."

> "Your admin team spends an hour on six invoices. I just built an automation that handles this for you in just two minutes. Watch to see how it works."

> "How long does your team spend matching PODs to jobs at the end of the day? I just took a photo of a paper POD on my phone. Watch it match to the right job automatically."

### 3. The "bold claim + proof" open
Lead with a specific number or stat, then back it up.

> "25 hours per week. That's what your admin team spends on order entry as a midsize haulage firm. Watch how this AI system handles it for you in just 10 seconds."

> "500 UK haulage companies went insolvent last year. Here's what the survivors have in common."

### 4. The personal story open (highest engagement)
First person, emotional, specific. These get the most likes and comments.

> "I've given up my career in tech to come back and solve the admin problem in UK haulage. Every time I hear a story like the one I'm about to tell you, I know I made the right choice."

> "I stopped taking tech clients to build automation for haulage. Here's why."

## Introduction pattern

George introduces himself the same way almost every time, usually 10-15 seconds in (never at the top):

> "I'm George, an automation specialist, and this is day [X] of showing UK haulage and fleet operators what's possible with AI."

Or the shorter version:
> "I'm George, an automation specialist."

Key: the intro comes AFTER the hook, never before it. The hook earns the right to introduce yourself.

## Sentence structure

- Sentences are short. Rarely more than 15 words.
- Active voice always. "The system reads the email" not "the email is read by the system."
- "So" is used as a transition constantly. It's George's natural connector word.
- "Here's how..." and "Here's what happened..." are his go-to reveal phrases.
- Questions are used to set up the next point: "But can it handle the messy emails? Let's find out."
- "Let's find out" is a signature phrase used to create anticipation.

## Rhythm and pacing

George's scripts follow a predictable rhythm:

1. **Hook** (0-5 seconds): Problem statement or bold claim
2. **Context** (5-15 seconds): Why this matters, who it's for
3. **Demo/Reveal** (15-45 seconds): Show the system working step by step
4. **Behind the scenes** (varies): "So here's what happened in the background..."
5. **The bigger point** (5-10 seconds): Business impact with specific numbers
6. **CTA** (3-5 seconds): "Follow along for tomorrow where I'll show you..."

## Transition phrases George uses constantly

- "So, here's what happened in the background."
- "So, here's how we built our AI automations to handle this."
- "Let's find out."
- "Watch what happens next."
- "But here's the really clever bit."
- "And the result? Well..."
- "Here's the bigger win."
- "But what happens if...? Follow along to find out."
- "Back to the video."
- "Okay, so..."

## How George explains technical things

He uses a consistent "what happened in the background" pattern:

> "So what actually happened in the background there? Well, once a delivery is marked as confirmed in your TMS, it automatically triggers this system. What it does is it automatically pulls the pre-agreed rate from the customer profile. From there, it builds the invoice and attaches the POD as proof."

Key elements:
- Step-by-step walkthrough using "first... then... from here..."
- Each step is one sentence
- No jargon that isn't haulage-specific (TMS is fine, "API endpoint" is not)
- Always ties back to what the viewer cares about: time saved, money saved, fewer mistakes

## Numbers and proof points George uses

George backs every claim with specifics. Never vague.

- "Less than 3 minutes from delivery confirmation to the invoice being in your customer's inbox"
- "75% reduction in invoicing time"
- "2.5 days per week saved"
- "10,000 combinations per second"
- "43% higher conversion rate"
- "£8,400 that's now sitting in your account and not theirs"
- "70 to 75% of your order emails can be processed automatically"

Pattern: [specific number] + [specific outcome] + [specific timeframe]

## CTA patterns

George's CTAs are always about the next video, never about selling:

- "Follow along for tomorrow where I'll show you how our quoting system can respond to quote requests after 5:00 p.m."
- "Check in tomorrow for day [X] where I'll show you..."
- "Follow along if you want to make 2026 your automated year."
- "Comment the word [X] below and I'll show you exactly how this system works."
- "If you're interested in getting a free working demo, then all you got to do is reach out to me via message."

## What George never does

- Never starts with "Hey guys" or "What's up everyone"
- Never uses "leverage", "synergy", "game-changing", "seamlessly"
- Never says "in today's fast-paced world" or "as a founder"
- Never uses em dashes
- Never lectures or talks down to the audience
- Never uses "we" when he means "I" (it's always first person)
- Never explains what AI is in abstract terms; always shows it doing something specific
- Never ends with "let me know your thoughts" or "drop a like"

## Tone markers

- **Peer-to-peer**: talks like a fellow operator, not an expert
- **Matter-of-fact**: states things plainly without hype
- **Empathetic about the pain**: "That's not dedication, it's a broken system, and it's completely fixable"
- **Urgency without pressure**: "That gap won't last" but never "act now before it's too late"
- **Conversational asides**: "I'll be honest", "And the worst part is", "Look"

## Format-specific notes

### Shorts (30-60 seconds, ~100-150 words)
- Hook is EVERYTHING. Must earn a stop-scroll in 2 seconds.
- One idea only. One demo. One point.
- End with a follow/comment CTA, never a link.
- George often uses "Let's find out" after posing a question as the hook.

### Long-form (5-18 minutes, ~1500-3000 words)
- Open loop in first 30 seconds (tease the outcome)
- Personal intro after the hook
- Step-by-step walkthrough is the core content
- Real case studies with company names and numbers
- Summary + next video CTA at the end
- Include [pause], [cut], and [B-roll: ...] markers
